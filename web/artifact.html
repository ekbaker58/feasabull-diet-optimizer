Targets: IOM Estimated Energy Requirement and Dietary Reference Intakes, the same method as FeasaBULL Calories. Nutrients: USDA SR28. Prices: Walmart, Oct 2026 (regular prices), or BLS U.S. city averages.

Amounts are as eaten: cooked rice, roasted chicken, baked potatoes. The shopping list converts them back to what you buy (dry rice, raw chicken) using USDA's cooked and raw values for each food.

The realistic plan follows the Dietary Guidelines for Americans 2025–2030 daily servings for your calorie level (protein foods, dairy, vegetables, fruits, whole grains, healthy fats), keeps at least half the grains whole and saturated fat under 10% of calories, spreads each food group across several foods, and prices in typical household food waste (USDA ERS). The textbook plan drops those rules: it is the classic Stigler/Dantzig diet problem, cheapest on paper and not how anyone eats.

For comparison, USDA's own cheapest official plan, the Thrifty Food Plan, is built from what Americans actually eat; it costs more because it includes mixed dishes, drinks and national-average prices.

Educational estimates for healthy people 14 and older, not medical advice. Unofficial student project, EGN4450 Team 9.
